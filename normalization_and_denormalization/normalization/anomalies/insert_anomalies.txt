-> Insertion anomalies occur when certain data cannot be inserted into a relation (table) without the presence of additional,
unnecessary or inconsistent data.

# Example : STUDENT_COURSE Relation
Suppose we have a single table STUDENT_COURSE :
+-----------+----------+-------------+------------------+
| StudentID | Name     | CourseCode  | CourseTitle      |
+-----------+----------+-------------+------------------+
| 101       | Alice    | CSE101      | Database Systems |
| 102       | Bob      | CSE102      | Networking       |
---------------------------------------------------------

Here, both Student data and Course data are stored together in the same table.

-> Insertion Anomaly Cases
> Case 1: Adding a new student (not yet enrolled in any course)
Suppose a new student Charlie (ID=103) joins, but hasn’t enrolled in any course. To insert him into STUDENT_COURSE, we are
forced to put NULL values for CourseCode and CourseTitle:
        | 103 | Charlie | NULL | NULL |
This causes NULL problems and violates integrity, because the relation mixes two different entities (Student + Course).

> Case 2: Adding a new course (with no students yet)
Suppose a new course CSE103 – Operating Systems is offered, but no one has enrolled yet. In this design, we cannot insert
the course alone into STUDENT_COURSE, because StudentID (part of primary key) cannot be NULL. We’d need to insert a fake
student record just to store this course.
This makes insertion of new courses impossible without unrelated student data.
