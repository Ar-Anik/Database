-> Data denormalization is the process of intentionally introducing redundancy into a database design by combining tables
or duplicating data. It’s the opposite of normalization, where the goal is to reduce redundancy and dependency. The main
purpose of denormalization is to improve query performance at the cost of extra storage and potential data inconsistency.

Q : Why Denormalize?
- Normalization ensures:
    1. Less redundancy
    2. Consistency
    3. Integrity
But it can also lead to:
    1. Many tables
    2. Complex queries (lots of joins)
    3. Slower read performance in some cases
Denormalization is often used in read-heavy systems, like reporting or analytics databases, where performance is
more important than strict consistency.

# Example
- Normalized Design (3NF):
1. Customers:
-----------------------
|CustomerID | Name    |
|-----------|---------|
|1          | Alice   |
|2          | Bob     |
-----------+-----------

2. Orders:
--------------------------------
OrderID | CustomerID | Product |
--------|------------|---------|
101     | 1          | Laptop  |
102     | 2          | Phone   |
--------------------------------

-> If we want CustomerName + Product, we must JOIN Customers and Orders.

- Denormalized Design:
1. Orders:
-----------------------------------------------
OrderID | CustomerID | CustomerName | Product |
--------|------------|--------------|---------|
101     | 1          | Alice        | Laptop  |
102     | 2          | Bob          | Phone   |
-----------------------------------------------

-> Here, CustomerName is stored in the Orders table (redundancy). This avoids a JOIN but increases storage and
risk of inconsistency (if a customer changes name, it must be updated in multiple places).
